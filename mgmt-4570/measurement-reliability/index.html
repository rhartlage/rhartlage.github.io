15-minute team activity

A diagnosis you can defend.

  1. 3 minutes: inspect the readings without references. Write one claim you can support and one you cannot.
  2. 4 minutes: reveal the references. Distinguish bias, repeatability, and operator disagreement.
  3. 5 minutes: predict which repair will help, try it, then test the other repairs. Explain why a metric may move even when it was not the target.
  4. 3 minutes: plan a check for your own process: operational definition, instrument, operators, repeated measurements, and a trusted reference.

Before collecting project data

Improve the measurement first.

  • Bias: the average difference from an accepted reference. Repeated agreement alone cannot establish accuracy.
  • Repeatability: variation when one operator measures the same item repeatedly under the same conditions.
  • Reproducibility: variation when measurement conditions change, such as the operator. This lab uses the mean B-minus-A difference as a simple indicator of systematic operator disagreement.
  • Different item, different question: the spread across all items mixes real item differences with measurement error. It is not a repeatability estimate.
How the simulation and calculations work

Model: reading = item reference + common bias + operator offset + random error. Operator A has offset −gap/2; B has +gap/2. Random errors are independent normal draws with mean zero and the stated model standard deviation. The five item references are exact in this simulation; real reference uncertainty must be considered.

Estimated average bias: average of all 30 (reading − reference) differences. Operator-specific biases use each operator's 15 differences.

Pooled repeatability: subtract each item/operator cell mean from its three readings, sum the 30 squared residuals, divide by 5 × 2 × (3 − 1) = 20 degrees of freedom, then take the square root. This excludes between-item and systematic between-operator mean differences.

Mean operator gap: average of the five (operator B mean − operator A mean) differences. It is signed, in millimeters, and is not a reproducibility standard deviation or a variance component.

Controlled comparison: repairs reuse the same underlying random draws, so a repair can be isolated. New sample changes the seed and creates a fresh paired comparison. Reset restores the case's original seed and clears repairs and reference reveal. A repaired study is a model comparison, not an independent validation study.

Limits: no full gage R&R ANOVA, percent GRR, acceptance thresholds, specification limits, process capability, long-term drift, operator-by-item interaction, instrument-to-instrument variation, or instrument resolution model. The controlled cases are illustrations, not a measurement-system certification. Real studies need a representative sample, randomized/blinded measurement order, appropriate repeats, and a method matched to their design.
