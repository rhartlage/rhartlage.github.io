Two different questions

Stable does not mean acceptable.

Control limits

Estimated from process data. “Is the process behaving consistently?” The x-bar chart tracks averages, not individual items.

Specification limits

Set by a customer or design requirement. “Does this individual item meet the requirement?” They do not move the control limits.

Use this in one existing chart lesson

  1. Predict: Begin with “Baseline continuation.” Describe what ordinary variation looks like.
  2. Compare: Change only the monitoring period. Explain why the reference stays fixed.
  3. Check: Work through one subgroup and recreate its result in a spreadsheet.
  4. Decide: Name a sensible investigation or measurement check. A signal does not identify the cause.
Assumptions, limitations & references

Examples are synthetic teaching data, deliberately simple, not measurements from a real organization. A practical baseline often uses 20–25 or more representative, rational subgroups. Screen a baseline for assignable causes and measurement problems before using it prospectively; do not remove inconvenient points just to force stability.

x-bar/R: continuous measurements, independent rational subgroups, equal subgroup size 2–10, and a suitable approximately normal within-subgroup measurement distribution. The range constants are rounded tabulated values. p: each unit is conforming or nonconforming, independent Bernoulli outcomes and a common underlying probability within a stable phase. Multiple defects on one unit need a different chart. Overdispersion, clustering, autocorrelation and changing definitions can invalidate these limits.

The default rule is a point strictly beyond a 3-sigma limit. The optional eight-point rule is a selected supplementary rule, not a full Western Electric/Nelson ruleset; more rules increase false alarms. Baseline flags are retrospective diagnostics; monitoring flags are comparisons to the fixed reference. No signal is not proof of control. Out-of-limit counts are not causal diagnoses.

References: NIST: x-bar and R charts; NIST: proportions control charts; NIST: control-chart rules and interpretation.
