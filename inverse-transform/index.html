Why it works

Probability becomes position.

For a continuous, strictly increasing CDF, F(X) = U. Because U fills the probability scale uniformly, the resulting X values follow the chosen distribution. A steep CDF assigns more draws to a small range of X.

F⁻¹(u) = inf {x : F(x) ≥ u}, for 0 < u < 1

Try it in class

Predict. Trace. Repeat.

  1. With Exponential, compare U = 0.50 and U = 0.95. Why does a small change near 1 create a large waiting time?
  2. Switch to Poisson. Move U slowly. Why can several probabilities generate the same whole-number count?

Endpoints, numerical methods, and references

Random draws use the midpoints of 2³² equally likely probability cells, so 0 and 1 are never drawn. The manual controls allow both endpoints to illustrate limits: Normal gives −∞ and +∞; Exponential gives 0 and +∞; Poisson with a positive mean gives 0 and +∞. Uniform and Binomial use their support limits. A zero-mean Poisson is always zero. Endpoint previews are never counted as random samples.

The Normal inverse is calculated numerically using a tail-stable CDF. Poisson uses a probability recurrence and a conservative tail cutoff; its mean is limited to 100 here. The CDF for a discrete distribution is a right-continuous step function: filled points belong to the step, open points do not. Dashed vertical jump guides are not additional attainable CDF values. Plots show a useful window and expand to include the current finite preview; the distribution itself is not truncated. Samples outside the visible window are reported below the plots.

Continuous histograms show count ÷ (total draws × bin width); discrete bars show count ÷ total draws. Sample SD uses n − 1. Changing the distribution or its parameters clears all samples. Up to 50,000 random draws can be held in this tab; refresh clears everything.
